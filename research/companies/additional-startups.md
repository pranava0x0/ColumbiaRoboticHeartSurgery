# Additional Cardiac Robotics Companies

Beyond the "big four" already covered (Intuitive/da Vinci, Medtronic Hugo, Corindus/CorPath, Verb/Ottava), this survey covers six more companies with real, verifiable cardiac relevance — spanning surgical robotics, robotic transcatheter structural heart repair, robotic endovascular/EP catheters, soft robotics for heart failure, and one academic autonomous-surgery research program. Each entry is honest about how directly the technology touches cardiac care versus how adjacent/aspirational it is.

---

## Stereotaxis (Genesis / GenesisX + MAGiC catheter)

- **Technology**: Robotic *magnetic* navigation, not a mechanical arm like da Vinci. The Genesis/GenesisX system uses external magnets to steer flexible catheter tips remotely inside the heart, controlled by the operator from a console outside the radiation field. GenesisX (FDA-cleared) integrates magnetic shielding into the unit itself, removing the need to shield an entire operating/cath lab wall — a major cost/installation advantage over the original Niobe-generation systems.
- **Cardiac relevance**: Direct and core. This is a cardiac electrophysiology (arrhythmia ablation) and interventional-cardiology robotic platform, not general surgery. In January 2026 the company won FDA approval for its **MAGiC** magnetic ablation catheter, the first robotically-driven ablation catheter of its kind, extending Genesis from navigation-only to full robotic ablation.
- **Investors & funding**: Publicly traded (NYSE American: STXS); market cap roughly $180M as of 2026. Not venture-backed at this stage.
- **Use-cases/deployments**: Legacy Niobe/Genesis systems installed at 100+ hospitals worldwide with 100,000+ historical procedures (mostly AFib and other arrhythmia ablation). Robotic catheter (MAGiC) revenue reported surpassing $1M in Q2 2026, up 270% sequentially, reflecting an early but accelerating commercial ramp.
- **Sales/commercial status**: Commercial, publicly reporting revenue (~$40M+ annual run rate projected for 2026), primarily electrophysiology labs rather than cardiac surgery ORs.
- **Company basics**: Founded 1990, HQ St. Louis, MO.
- **2026 development**: On July 9, 2026, Stereotaxis completed its acquisition of Robocath (see below) for $20M upfront plus up to $25M in milestones, consolidating two of the companies on this list into one robotic endovascular/EP platform.
- Sources: [MedTech Dive, MAGiC FDA approval](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/stereotaxis-fda-approval-robot-catheter/808941/), [Stereotaxis IR release](https://ir.stereotaxis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/stereotaxis-receives-fda-approval-magic-ablation-catheter), [Stereotaxis About](https://www.stereotaxis.com/about-us/), [Robocath acquisition completion](https://ir.stereotaxis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/stereotaxis-completes-acquisition-robocath/)

## Robocath (R-One) — now part of Stereotaxis

- **Technology**: Robotic-assisted percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) — a catheter-driving robot in the same clinical category as Corindus/CorPath, controlled via joysticks from a shielded cockpit to advance guidewires, balloons, and stents. R-One+ is the current-generation platform.
- **Cardiac relevance**: Direct — interventional cardiology (PCI/angioplasty for coronary artery disease), the same procedure class as CorPath, positioning it as a direct European-originated competitor to Siemens Healthineers' system rather than a cardiac *surgery* robot.
- **Investors & funding**: Founded 2009 in Rouen, France, by Philippe Bencteux. Raised in stages — roughly €1.8M (2013–15), €6.4M (2017), €40M Series C (April 2020, led by MicroPort Scientific). Total lifetime funding estimated $75–90M depending on source (PitchBook/CB Insights). **Acquired outright by Stereotaxis** for $20M upfront + up to $25M in milestones, deal completed July 9, 2026.
- **Use-cases/deployments**: CE Mark obtained February 2019; first patient case September 2019 (France). In clinical use across Europe, Africa, and China; validated in the multicenter R-EVOLUTION study.
- **Sales/commercial status**: Commercial in CE Mark geographies; no FDA clearance reported as of this research. Now folded into Stereotaxis's roadmap for "next-generation" interventional cardiology and neurointerventional robotics rather than operating as an independent company.
- **Company basics**: HQ Rouen, France; founded 2009.
- Sources: [MassDevice, CE Mark](https://www.massdevice.com/robocath-wins-ce-mark-for-r-one-coronary-platform/), [Robocath company page](https://www.robocath.com/company/), [Stereotaxis acquisition announcement](https://ir.stereotaxis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/stereotaxis-announces-definitive-agreement-acquire-robocath), [Tech.eu Series C](https://tech.eu/2020/04/30/robocatch-series-c/)

## Capstan Medical

- **Technology**: A dedicated **robotic transcatheter structural heart platform** — not a general surgical robot repurposed for cardiac use, but built specifically to deliver and precisely position nitinol-based valve implants via catheter, replacing manual fluoroscopy-guided placement with robotic steering and positioning under the physician's control.
- **Cardiac relevance**: Direct and specific — the platform's first target is transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR), with a tricuspid valve replacement implant also in development. This is squarely a valve-repair-specialist company rather than adjacent tech.
- **Investors & funding**: $31.4M raised in 2023; $110M Series C led by Eclipse in December 2024, joined by Yu Galaxy and, notably, **Intuitive Ventures** (Intuitive Surgical's investment arm) — a sign the da Vinci maker sees this as complementary rather than purely competitive.
- **Use-cases/deployments**: First-in-human robot-assisted transcatheter mitral valve replacements completed and announced March 2025 — described as the first clinical use of a minimally invasive robotic structural heart platform. A second-generation system is planned for online use sometime in 2026.
- **Sales/commercial status**: Pre-commercial/early clinical stage; no regulatory clearance reported yet.
- **Company basics**: Founded 2020, HQ Santa Cruz, CA.
- Sources: [MedTech Dive, first-in-human](https://www.massdevice.com/capstan-medical-first-in-human-robot-assisted-transcatheter-mitral-valve-replacement/), [MedTech Dive, $110M raise](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/Capstan-Medical-funding-round-heart-valve-robot/735404/), [HIT Consultant, 2023 raise](https://hitconsultant.net/2023/08/09/robotics-heart-valve-startup-capstan-medical-raises-31-4m/)

## Moray Medical (Coral platform)

- **Technology**: An interventional robotics platform built around microfluidics-driven catheter steering plus an augmented-reality digital interface for the operator, aimed at simplifying minimally invasive cardiovascular interventions generally, starting with transcatheter mitral valve repair (TMVR) — a different mechanical approach (microfluidic actuation + AR) than Capstan's implant-delivery robot or Corindus/Robocath's PCI catheter robots.
- **Cardiac relevance**: Direct — structural heart/mitral repair is the explicit first indication.
- **Investors & funding**: Seed round of $3.3M led by 415 Capital (announced January 2021), extended to a total of $5.7M with participation from Heal Capital and angel investors including Dr. Fred St. Goar (inventor of the MitraClip). No larger rounds were found in this research pass, and no financing news was found more recent than 2021 — treat Moray's current funding/operational status as unconfirmed rather than assume it has scaled.
- **Use-cases/deployments**: As of the most recent information found (around 2021, with a passing 2025 reference), the company had completed live-animal studies and indicated the FDA had validated a 510(k) pathway that would not require large-scale clinical trials before a first-in-human step — but no confirmed first-in-human procedure, clearance, or subsequent funding was found in this pass. This is the least-verified entry in this file; treat its current status as stale/unclear rather than current.
- **Sales/commercial status**: Pre-commercial; development stage as last reported.
- **Company basics**: Founded circa 2020–21; CEO Mark Barrish.
- Sources: [MedTech Innovator, seed](https://medtechinnovator.org/interventional-robotics-startup-moray-medical-secures-3-3-million-seed-financing-led-by-415-capital/), [Med-Tech News, extended seed](https://www.med-technews.com/news/medtech-merger-acquisition-finance-and-investment-news/interventional-robotics-developer-closes-5-7m-seed-round/), [Heal Capital announcement](https://medium.com/heal-capital/time-to-act-announcing-moray-medical-be4227c2b7b8)

## CorInnova (EpicHeart)

- **Technology**: Not a surgical robot at all in the conventional sense — a **soft robotic direct cardiac compression device**. A thin-film, pneumatically actuated sleeve is delivered minimally invasively and wraps around both ventricles, then rhythmically inflates/deflates with air to augment the heart's own pumping (systolic support) for heart failure patients. Critically, it is **non-blood-contacting** — a structural differentiator from LVADs and other blood pumps, aimed at avoiding the clotting/bleeding risks those devices carry.
- **Cardiac relevance**: Direct — it is a heart-failure therapy device, not a surgical tool. Its relevance to a robotic *surgery* microsite is as an adjacent robotic cardiac technology rather than a competing surgical platform.
- **Investors & funding**: Backed by private investors plus non-dilutive support from NHLBI and NSF; received a $6.1M investment from the UK's Wellcome Trust in 2015. A JLABS @ TMC (Texas Medical Center) resident company.
- **Use-cases/deployments**: Tested in roughly 20 large-animal studies, primarily at Texas Heart Institute and Texas A&M University. No human clinical trial results were found in this research pass.
- **Sales/commercial status**: Pre-clinical/research stage; not commercially available.
- **Company basics**: Founded 2004 in Houston, TX, commercializing technology from Dr. John Criscione's Cardiac Mechanics Laboratory at Texas A&M University (not Rice, despite that association appearing in some secondary sources).
- Sources: [MassDevice, Wellcome Trust raise](https://www.massdevice.com/corinnova-raises-6m-epicheart-soft-robotic-heart-failure-treatment/), [DAIC, patent coverage](https://www.dicardiology.com/content/corinnova-awarded-seminal-patent-minimally-invasively-delivered-soft-robotic-heart-device), [TMC News](https://www.tmc.edu/news/2019/02/two-tmc-backed-startups-aim-to-change-the-way-we-treat-failing-hearts/)

## Johns Hopkins STAR (Smart Tissue Autonomous Robot) — research program, not a company

- **Technology**: An autonomous surgical robot that performs suturing tasks with minimal human input — the surgeon defines the task, and STAR tracks soft-tissue deformation in real time and adapts its suturing plan autonomously, rather than being teleoperated stitch-by-stitch like da Vinci.
- **Cardiac relevance**: **Honest caveat — this is not a cardiac-specific program.** STAR's published, demonstrated procedures to date are intestinal anastomosis and, most recently (2025), a fully autonomous laparoscopic gallbladder removal on a simulated patient — both general-surgery/soft-tissue applications, not cardiac ones. It is included here because autonomous suturing is the technology category most directly relevant to a future autonomous coronary anastomosis (the core technical challenge in CABG), and it is the most advanced public research effort in that direction — but as of this research pass it has not been applied to cardiac tissue or vessels.
- **Investors & funding**: Federally funded academic research (Johns Hopkins University, led by Axel Krieger's lab), not a venture-funded startup. No evidence of a commercial spinout company was found in this research pass.
- **Use-cases/deployments**: Pre-clinical, animal and simulated-patient studies only. The research team has stated an aim of first-in-human studies within roughly five years as of 2022 reporting.
- **Sales/commercial status**: Not commercial; research-stage only.
- **Company basics**: N/A — university research program, Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, MD.
- Sources: [Johns Hopkins Hub, 2025 autonomous surgery](https://hub.jhu.edu/2025/07/09/robot-performs-first-realistic-surgery-without-human-help/), [Baltimore Sun](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2022/03/22/johns-hopkins-engineers-build-robot-to-perform-surgery-without-a-doctor/), [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/johns-hopkins-teaches-robot-to-perform-a-gallbladder-removal-on-a-realistic-patient/)

## Landscape summary

None of these six challenge da Vinci's position in *cardiac surgery* specifically. None has FDA clearance for CABG, valve surgery, or any open/endoscopic cardiac operation, and da Vinci remains the only robot cleared for cardiac use in the US.

What this wider net shows instead is that robotic investment in "the heart" is concentrated **around** surgery rather than inside it. That includes robotic catheter-based platforms for arrhythmia ablation and PCI (Stereotaxis, absorbing Robocath) and robotic transcatheter valve delivery aiming to make structural heart repair as precise as surgery without opening the chest (Capstan, Moray). It also spans soft-robotic heart-failure therapy that is not surgery at all (CorInnova), plus academic work on autonomous suturing (Johns Hopkins STAR) that is general-surgery-proven but not yet cardiac-proven.

The clearest signal of where this is heading is Intuitive Surgical's own venture arm backing Capstan — the incumbent surgical robot maker investing in adjacent transcatheter robotics rather than competing with it directly. For a program built around da Vinci-based robotic CABG, these companies are not near-term competitive threats. They are the neighboring frontiers (EP, PCI, structural heart, heart failure, autonomy research) that a hospital's broader cardiac robotics strategy may eventually track.
