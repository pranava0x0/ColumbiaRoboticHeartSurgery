# Intuitive Surgical: Robotic Cardiac Surgery

## Company Overview

**Headquarters:** Sunnyvale, California, USA
**Status:** Public company (NASDAQ: ISRG)
**Founded:** 1995, by Frederic H. Moll, John G. Freund, and Robert G. Younge
**Revenue:** ~$11.0 billion (FY 2026 run-rate), $10.1 billion actual FY2025 (instruments/accessories + service + operating lease + system sales)
**Market cap:** roughly $140–190 billion through 2026, depending on the trading day (52-week range $414.30–$603.88; all-time closing high $610.45 on 2025-01-22) — up from ~$64.6 billion five years earlier, a ~3x gain over that window
**Primary Product:** da Vinci Surgical System (minimally invasive robotic surgery platform)

Intuitive Surgical is the dominant manufacturer of surgical robots globally, with an estimated 60%+ share of the soft-tissue robotic surgery market. The da Vinci System remains the most widely deployed robotic platform cleared for general surgical use, though competitors (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava, CMR Surgical Versius) are now shipping commercial systems in adjacent specialties. ([Rijnberk Invest Insights: "A 60%+ Market Share That Comes with 85% Recurring Revenue"](https://rijnberkinvestinsights.substack.com/p/intuitive-surgical-a-60-market-share))

### Origins

The core technology traces to telepresence/minimally invasive surgery research at SRI International (with Stanford ties) in the late 1980s–early 1990s, originally explored for battlefield telesurgery applications funded by DARPA/the U.S. Army. Frederic Moll (a surgeon, then an executive at Guidant) and John Freund (an Acuson ultrasound veteran) negotiated the IP rights out of SRI in 1994–95 and founded Intuitive Surgical with Robert Younge, also an Acuson alum. ([Startup Intros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/intuitive-surgical))

### Acquisitions

**Important correction to a common misconception:** the $3.4B (not $3.6B) **Auris Health** acquisition in 2019 was made by **Johnson & Johnson (Ethicon)**, not Intuitive Surgical — despite Auris having been co-founded by Intuitive's own co-founder Frederic Moll. This is a real point of confusion because of the Moll connection, but Auris (maker of the Monarch bronchoscopy platform) has been a J&J subsidiary since April 2019, making it a *competitor* asset, not an Intuitive one. ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-announces-completion-of-acquisition-of-auris-health-inc), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michelatindera/2019/02/14/intuitive-surgical-stock-robot-surgery-da-vinci-alphabet-jnj-ceo-gary-guthart/))

Intuitive's own actual acquisitions have been smaller and more consolidative:
- **Computer Motion (2003)** — the single most strategically important deal in the company's history. Intuitive and its only real early competitor (maker of the AESOP and Zeus surgical robot systems) had been locked in overlapping patent-infringement litigation. They merged on June 30, 2003 (Computer Motion holders got 32% of the combined company, Intuitive holders 68%), which ended the litigation, consolidated the foundational IP for robotic MIS under one roof, and effectively removed Intuitive's only meaningful rival — a major factor in the company's subsequent multi-decade market dominance in soft-tissue robotic surgery. ([Intuitive press release](https://isrg.intuitive.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-surgical-and-computer-motion-close-merger-intuitive))
- **Orpheus Medical (Feb 2020)** — surgical video/informatics connectivity company, expanding Intuitive's data/analytics platform (feeds into products like Case Insights and Custom Hospital Analytics).
- **da Vinci/Ion distribution business from ab medica, Abex, and Excelencia Robótica (completed March 2026)** — Intuitive brought several of its international third-party distributors (covering parts of Europe and Latin America) in-house, a commercial-structure move rather than a technology acquisition.

Intuitive has **not** made a large, splashy M&A play the way J&J (Auris, $3.4B), Medtronic, or Stryker (Mako) have — its growth has been overwhelmingly organic, funded by recurring instrument/service revenue reinvested into R&D and regulatory expansion (e.g., the years-long push to get da Vinci 5 cardiac-cleared).

## Technology: da Vinci Architecture in Depth

### Platform Generations
| System | Introduced | Key Architecture |
|---|---|---|
| **da Vinci Si** | 2009 | Dual-console capable, patient-cart mounted arms, precursor generation |
| **da Vinci Xi** | 2014 | Overhead **boom-mounted** arm architecture — arms hang from a rotating boom rather than a fixed cart, enabling anatomical access from nearly any position without repositioning the whole cart mid-case; thinner arms with redesigned joints for greater range of motion; integrated table motion; laser-targeting for portal placement |
| **da Vinci X** | 2017 | A lower-cost, more compact version of Xi-generation technology aimed at expanding access to systems with smaller capital budgets |
| **da Vinci SP (Single Port)** | 2018 (urology-focused initially) | A single ~2.5cm cannula delivers a fully articulating 3D endoscope plus three wristed instruments through one port; distinct architecture from the multi-arm Xi/X |
| **da Vinci 5** | Cleared 2024 (general), cardiac clearance Jan 26, 2026 | Latest flagship: same core Xi-style multi-arm architecture but with **~10,000x more computing power**, new console ergonomics, an integrated tower (E200 generator, new insufflation/smoke evacuation), heated scope, and — the headline feature — **true force feedback** |

### Console & Surgeon-Side Controls
The surgeon sits at a separate console away from the patient, viewing a stereoscopic 3D-HD display and manipulating two master controllers with fingertip precision. Hand movements are scaled down and tremor is filtered out. da Vinci 5 redesigned the hand controllers specifically to transmit the new force-feedback data.

### EndoWrist Instrumentation
Proprietary wristed instruments (8mm and 12mm diameters common; smaller-diameter instruments exist for some platforms) provide **7 degrees of freedom**, exceeding the natural range of motion of a human wrist, which is what enables suturing and fine dissection through small ports. Instruments are usage-limited (a "razor/blade" consumable — see Business Model below) and are re-engineered for each new platform generation while maintaining broad backward compatibility.

### Imaging
All current-generation systems use immersive **3D-HD vision**, magnified well beyond unassisted human vision. da Vinci 5's imaging pipeline benefits from the massive computing uplift for real-time image enhancement.

### Haptics / Force Feedback — the biggest recent architectural change
Every da Vinci generation before da Vinci 5 was **haptics-free**: surgeons relied entirely on visual cues to judge tissue tension and suture force, a long-cited limitation versus open surgery. **da Vinci 5 is the first da Vinci platform with true force feedback (FFB)** — real-time transmission of instrument-tip forces to the surgeon's hands via redesigned console controllers and FFB-enabled instruments. Preclinical and early clinical data (e.g., in colorectal and thoracic surgery) show surgeons using FFB instruments exert measurably less tissue force than with non-FFB Xi instruments, a plausible driver of reduced tissue trauma. **Cardiac clearance as of January 2026 covers only non-force-feedback instruments** — Intuitive is still pursuing cardiac clearance for the FFB instrument portfolio, expected to roll out over time. ([Medical Design & Outsourcing](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/intuitive-da-vinci-5-design-changes-force-feedback-haptic/), [PMC thoracic force analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12464090/))

### AI / Data Platforms
- **Iris** — FDA-cleared (Feb 2019) augmented-reality imaging tool, originally for kidney procedures, used in pre-surgical planning to visualize anatomy/approach.
- **Case Insights** — an AI layer that evaluates da Vinci system data, kinematic movement, and case video to generate objective performance insights for surgeon training and skill development, drawing on a database Intuitive describes as covering **10+ million cases**.
- **Custom Hospital Analytics** — a program helping hospital customers track clinical, financial, and operational metrics tied to their robotic program (use rates, block time, cost per case), useful for justifying and optimizing a new program internally.
- da Vinci 5's ~10,000x compute increase over Xi is explicitly positioned by Intuitive as the enabler for real-time intraoperative AI guidance and safety features going forward, not just faster graphics.

### da Vinci SP and Cardiac Relevance
The single-port SP system is architecturally distinct (one 3D endoscope + three wristed instruments through a single ~2.5cm cannula) and has been used off-label/investigationally in **cardiac electrophysiology and rhythm-device contexts** — e.g., a published case of SP-based robotically-assisted **epicardial lead placement for cardiac resynchronization therapy** via a 4cm minithoracotomy, and early feasibility work on SP-based **bilateral internal thoracic artery harvesting**. However, SP is **not** the platform behind Intuitive's newly cleared (Jan 2026) mainstream cardiac indications — those clearances are on **da Vinci 5**, which uses the multi-arm Xi-style architecture, not SP. SP's cardiac role today is narrower and more experimental (lead placement, some EP/ablation-adjacent work) rather than valve repair or bypass. In markets like Japan, SP's 2022 regulatory approval explicitly **excluded** cardiac and intercostal procedures. ([PMC epicardial lead study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13100718/))

## Use Cases & Deployments

### Installed Base
As of **June 30, 2026**, the worldwide da Vinci installed base reached **11,710 systems**, up **12%** year-over-year from 10,488 a year earlier. North America remains the largest installed region; Asia-Pacific is the fastest-growing. Installed-base density in mature markets (US, parts of Europe) is approaching **12–18 systems per million population**, while many emerging markets remain under **2 systems per million** — a large runway for continued placement growth, especially outside the U.S. ([SEC earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1035267/000103526726000047/q226ex-991earningsrelease.htm))

### Procedure Volume
- **2025 total worldwide procedures (da Vinci + Ion): over 3.2 million**, serving over 20 million patients cumulatively.
- da Vinci procedures alone: **over 3.1 million in 2025, up 18%** (15% U.S. growth, 23% international growth).
- Ion (lung biopsy robotic bronchoscopy platform) procedures grew ~51% in 2025.
- **2026 guidance: 13–15% worldwide da Vinci procedure growth.**
- Historically (2016 data), procedure mix was dominated by **gynecology (44%)**, **general surgery (33%)**, and **urology (19%)** — cardiac was not yet a meaningful category. More recent commentary puts gyn around ~20% of mix as general surgery has grown its share. ([Intuitive Surgical FY2016 Form 10-K — SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001035267/000103526717000021/isrg-20161231x10k.htm)) **Cardiac-specific procedure volume as a percentage of total is not broken out by Intuitive in public disclosures** — as of the January 2026 cardiac clearance, cardiac is explicitly being rolled out as a small, controlled cohort of sites in 2026 rather than a mature high-volume category. This is a new and small (not yet material) part of Intuitive's business, which matters directly for a program considering entering it now — you'd be an early adopter relative to Intuitive's own commercial ramp, not joining an established high-volume category the way urology or gyn robotic programs are.

### Cardiac-Specific Rollout (as of early 2026)
- FDA cardiac clearance for da Vinci 5 came **January 26, 2026**, covering: mitral valve repair, mitral valve replacement, tricuspid valve repair, internal mammary artery (IMA) mobilization for revascularization, PFO/ASD closure, left atrial appendage closure/occlusion, atrial myxoma excision, and epicardial pacing lead placement — all using **non-force-feedback** instruments initially.
- Intuitive is deliberately **not** doing a broad simultaneous rollout: "a limited number of U.S. sites" began working with Intuitive through 2026 to establish da Vinci 5 cardiac programs, with **broader volume scaling starting April 1, 2026**. CEO Dave Rosa has described this as a purposely cautious, training-intensive rollout given cardiac surgery's complexity.
- da Vinci 5 cardiac clearance in **Korea** already exists; Intuitive is pursuing approvals in Europe and elsewhere, in some cases including the force-feedback instrument set.
- Competitively, this cardiac indication is currently a **differentiator versus Medtronic (Hugo) and J&J (Ottava)**, whose newer robotic platforms (launched 2025–2026) have focused on other specialties rather than cardiac. ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/intuitive-fda-510k-clearances-cardiac-procedures-da-vinci-5/), [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/Intuitive-Surgical-Q4-earnings-da-Vinci-cardiac-surgery-ASCs/810296/))

### TECAB & Coronary Bypass

**Historic milestone:** Columbia Presbyterian Medical Center (New York-Presbyterian/Columbia) performed the **first totally endoscopic coronary artery bypass in the United States** using da Vinci in January 2002 — a landmark directly relevant to a Columbia-affiliated program.

**FDA status:** The FDA's original intracardiac clearance (2002) did **not** explicitly encompass CABG, and the January 2026 cardiac clearance list above still does not appear to explicitly list full TECAB/CABG as a cleared indication — IMA mobilization (a component step of CABG) is cleared, but robotic anastomosis for full TECAB continues to be performed largely under surgeon judgment/off-label use and institutional protocols rather than a dedicated FDA CABG indication. This is an important regulatory nuance for anyone building a program around robotic bypass specifically, as distinct from robotic valve surgery.

**Institutional experience:**
- University of Chicago Medicine: **>2,000 robotic TECAB procedures** performed — the deepest single-center TECAB experience cited in public sources.
- Global cumulative figure (as of prior research): **>140,000 robotic-assisted cardiac procedures across 51 countries** (this figure spans the broader "robotic cardiac" category, not TECAB alone, and predates the 2026 clearance expansion).
- **Southeast Asia's first robotic TECAB** was performed at Harapan Kita National Heart Centre in Indonesia in **November 2024**, using the SSI Mantra system (a non-Intuitive competitor robot) rather than da Vinci — notable because it shows the region's cardiac-robotics adoption isn't exclusively an Intuitive story.
- **Europe:** robotic coronary bypass has been performed for over 20 years but adoption remains limited worldwide; a 2022 EACTS (European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery)-endorsed Robotic Cardiothoracic Surgery Taskforce paper notes a slight recent uptick in Europe but flags **high procedural cost, limited platform availability, and absence of dedicated training programs** as the main adoption barriers, with the technique still largely confined to tertiary academic centers. ([Oxford Academic/ICVTS](https://academic.oup.com/icvts/article/35/4/ivac108/6588518))

### Notable Cardiac Robotic Programs Beyond Columbia/UChicago
- **Cleveland Clinic** — one of the most aggressive robotic cardiac programs nationally. Acquired a **second dedicated cardiac robot (da Vinci 5)** in autumn 2025, making its Main Campus one of the only sites worldwide with two dedicated cardiac robots. Robotically-assisted cardiothoracic case volume rose **50% year-over-year in the first half of 2026**, with cumulative robot-assisted cardiothoracic cases approaching **3,000 by mid-2026**. Also launched a robotically-assisted **lung transplantation** program (first case May 2026) and runs a dedicated CME course, "State of the Art Robotics in Cardiac and Thoracic Surgery."
- **Mayo Clinic** — long-running robotic mitral program; a **10-year follow-up of 843 patients** reported **93% freedom from reoperation** after robotic mitral valve repair, among the strongest long-term outcomes data in the field.
- **Cedars-Sinai** — known for minimizing incision size in robotic cardiac cases (described as incisions "no larger than the short side of a driver's license"), associated with reduced postoperative pain and faster recovery.

([Cleveland Clinic Consult QD: "The Right Time for Robotics in Cardiothoracic Surgery"](https://consultqd.clevelandclinic.org/the-right-time-for-robotics-in-cardiothoracic-surgery), [ACS Bulletin: "Robotics Integration Ushers in New Era of Cardiac Surgery"](https://www.facs.org/for-medical-professionals/news-publications/news-and-articles/bulletin/2025/october-2025-volume-110-issue-9/robotics-integration-ushers-in-new-era-of-cardiac-surgery/))

### Clinical Outcomes Data: Mitral Repair
The key foundational U.S. multicenter trial (FDA phase II) enrolled **112 patients across 10 centers**, all with moderate-to-severe mitral regurgitation, using peripheral cardiopulmonary bypass through a 4–5cm right minithoracotomy:
- **No operative deaths**, no conversions due to system malfunction, no conversions to larger thoracotomy/sternotomy, no strokes, no device-related complications.
- At 1-month follow-up: **8.0%** of patients (9/112) had grade ≥2 recurrent mitral regurgitation; **5.4%** (6/112) required reoperation.
- Average leaflet repair time: 36.7 min; annuloplasty time: 39.6 min; total robotic time: 77.9 min; aortic cross-clamp time: 2.1 hrs; CPB time: 2.8 hrs.
This early trial data, combined with Mayo's 93%-freedom-from-reoperation 10-year outcomes, forms the core evidence base institutions cite when building the clinical case for a robotic mitral program. ([JTCVS multicenter trial](https://www.jtcvs.org/article/S0022-5223(04)01172-9/fulltext))

## Commercial / Sales Model

### The "Razor and Blades" Model
Intuitive's business is built on three revenue streams, and the recurring two now dominate:
1. **System sales** (the "razor") — capital equipment sale/lease of the da Vinci unit itself.
2. **Instruments & accessories** (the "blades") — EndoWrist instruments are usage-limited/single-use-cycle consumables that must be repurchased continually as the installed base performs more procedures.
3. **Service** — annual maintenance/service contracts on installed systems.

### 2025 Revenue Detail
- Instruments & accessories revenue: **$6.02 billion** (up 19% YoY from $5.08B in 2024), driven by procedure volume growth.
- Service revenue: **$1.57 billion** (up 20% YoY from $1.31B).
- Operating lease revenue: **$874 million**.
- Combined recurring revenue (instruments + service + lease): **~$8.46 billion**, i.e., the large majority of total company revenue — historically cited around **70%+ of revenue from recurring sources**, versus system sales.
- Illustrative historical split (2016): system sales were $792M (29% of revenue) with instruments/accessories accounting for over half of revenue and service ~19% — the recurring share has only grown since.

### Pricing
- A da Vinci system typically runs **~$1–2.5 million** depending on configuration; the newest **da Vinci 5** is priced around **$1.8–2.5 million**.
- Annual service/maintenance contracts: **~$100,000–$170,000 per year** per system.
- Per-procedure instrument/accessory costs: roughly **$700–$3,200 per case** depending on procedure complexity and instrument set, with Intuitive estimated to sell around **$1,900 in replacement parts per operation** on average.

This economics matters directly for a hospital evaluating a program: the capital outlay for the robot itself is a one-time (if large) cost, but the ongoing instrument/service spend scales directly with procedure volume — meaning the program's cost profile is very sensitive to case volume ramp. Intuitive is rolling out cardiac cautiously with a small initial site cohort rather than opening it to every existing da Vinci customer at once.

## Relevance to Hospital Programs (e.g., Columbia)

1. **Established ecosystem, new cardiac chapter.** Columbia has 20+ years of institutional credibility in robotic cardiac surgery (the 2002 first-U.S.-TECAB milestone), but the **formal FDA cardiac clearance for da Vinci 5 is brand new (Jan 2026)** and Intuitive is deliberately limiting the initial rollout to a small site cohort. A program positioning itself now is effectively joining a ground-floor commercial rollout, not a mature category — this is a genuine point of differentiation/timing to emphasize.

2. **Equipment dominance, with a caveat.** da Vinci remains the only platform with FDA cardiac clearance as of 2026, but competitors (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava) are actively developing cardiac-capable systems, so the "only option" framing has a shelf life.

3. **Instrumentation gap is real and current.** The Jan 2026 cardiac clearance covers only **non-force-feedback** instruments — the safety/precision benefits of da Vinci 5's headline force-feedback feature are not yet available for cardiac cases, which is a legitimate limitation to note rather than gloss over.

4. **Outcomes data exists but is thinner for CABG than for mitral repair.** Published trial-grade outcomes are strongest for **mitral valve repair** (multicenter FDA trial + Mayo's 10-year data). TECAB/CABG evidence is more institutional-experience-based (UChicago's >2,000 cases) than randomized/trial-grade, and CABG itself sits in more of a regulatory gray zone (IMA mobilization is cleared; full robotic anastomosis largely is not an explicit indication) — useful nuance for setting expectations with a Columbia audience.

5. **Recurring-revenue economics favor high, consistent case volume.** Because instrument and service costs scale per-case, a program's cost-effectiveness argument depends heavily on ramping and sustaining procedure volume — relevant to any internal business case a hospital administration would want to see.
