# Verb Surgical → Ottava: Google/Alphabet & J&J Surgical Robotics

## Origins & Ownership History

**Verb Surgical Inc.** was announced in March 2015 as a strategic collaboration between **Ethicon** (J&J's surgical devices unit) and **Google Life Sciences**, then formally launched as an independent company on December 10, 2015, in partnership with **Verily Life Sciences LLC** (Google Life Sciences had renamed itself Verily earlier that year as it spun out under the Alphabet umbrella) ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-announces-formation-of-verb-surgical-inc-in-collaboration-with-verily); [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2015/12/alphabets-verily-and-jj-name-medical-device-joint-venture-verb-surgical/)).

The JV was capitalized by **Ethicon, Johnson & Johnson Innovation–JJDC Inc., and Verily**; reporting at the time put total investment at roughly **$500 million**, with Alphabet and J&J as the primary equity holders alongside other undisclosed investors. Neither company has ever published the exact equity split. Both parents contributed in-kind assets rather than only cash: Ethicon brought surgical-instrumentation IP and in-process R&D, while Verily contributed machine-vision, imaging-analysis, and data-analytics technology plus managerial and engineering talent ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-announces-formation-of-verb-surgical-inc-in-collaboration-with-verily)). Scott Huennekens (formerly of Volcano Corp.) was installed as founding President and CEO.

**Verily's exit (2019–2020):** On December 20, 2019, J&J announced an agreement to acquire Verily's remaining stake in Verb Surgical outright, with the deal expected to close in H1 2020; it did close in 2020. **Financial terms were not disclosed** by either party — no purchase price, valuation, or buyout multiple has ever been made public ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/jj-takes-over-verb-surgical-as-verily-exits-robotics-partnership/569504/); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/johnson-johnson-buys-out-verily-stake-verb-surgical/)). Verb Surgical was folded into J&J's surgery/robotics organization and effectively ceased to exist as a separate entity; the technology continued under what became the **Ottava** program inside J&J MedTech (Ethicon).

Around the same period, J&J's 2019 acquisition of **Auris Health** (~$3.4B, a separate deal) brought **Dr. Frederic Moll** — co-founder of Intuitive Surgical and inventor of the da Vinci platform — into J&J as **Chief Robotics Development Officer**, giving him direct oversight of the soft-tissue robotics program that Ottava became. Moll left J&J in 2023 to return to early-stage medtech investing ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/fred-moll-joins-ss-innovations-board/)).

**Current status:** Ottava is wholly owned and developed by Johnson & Johnson MedTech (Ethicon), with no remaining Alphabet/Verily/Google involvement of any kind.

## Investors / Financial History Since 2020

J&J does not break out Ottava-specific R&D spend in its financial disclosures, but two data points quantify the program's cost:

- **Q3 2021 impairment**: J&J recorded a **$900 million partial in-process R&D charge** tied to a roughly two-year delay in the Ottava timeline (technical development setbacks plus COVID-era supply-chain disruption), disclosed on its Q3 2021 earnings call ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/jj-q3-earnings-covid-electives-ottava-delay-robotics/608451/); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/jj-delaying-ottava-surgical-robot-by-2-years/)). That charge alone signals a program capitalized well into the billions of dollars.
- J&J has stated Ottava is expected to become a **growth contributor starting in 2028** — accounting for FDA clearance, manufacturing ramp, and market adoption lead time — meaning most of the investment to date is sunk cost against a multi-year-out payback ([PatSnap roadmap summary](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/jj-surgical-robotics-velys-monarch-ottava-roadmap/)).
- Ottava sits inside J&J MedTech's broader robotics bet alongside **Monarch** (flexible robotics for bronchoscopy/urology) and **Velys** (orthopedic robotics, which contributed to $1.59B in knee-related revenue growth in a recent period). No company disclosure breaks out Ottava's R&D line separately from the MedTech segment total.
- **No pricing, placement, or revenue targets have been disclosed** for Ottava as of the July 2026 clearance ([Yahoo Finance/MedTech Dive coverage](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/j-j-medtech-jumps-surgical-163910283.html)).

## Technology in Detail

**Architecture — table-integrated, not cart-based.** Ottava's defining departure from da Vinci is mechanical: instead of a separate mobile cart/boom that is wheeled to the patient and docked, **four robotic arms are built directly into a standard-size operating table**. J&J calls it "the world's first table-integrated soft-tissue robotic system." Each arm has nine joints for range of motion and collision avoidance, and the arms **fold and stow beneath the table** when not in use, rather than occupying floor space around the patient. J&J states this cuts OR footprint by roughly **30–50% versus boom- or cart-mounted competitors** (some coverage cites a specific 34% figure against da Vinci 5) ([J&J MedTech system page](https://www.jnjmedtech.com/en-US/products/robotics/ottava-robotic-surgical-system/system/); [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/jj-ottava-robotic-surgery-intuitive/699272/)).

**"Twin Motion."** A signature feature that synchronizes movement between the operating table and the robotic arms as a single coordinated system — the table can reposition the patient (e.g., to access a different abdominal quadrant) while the arms track the motion, reducing the need to undock and redock instruments mid-case, which is a real workflow pain point on cart-based systems.

**Instrumentation.** Ottava exclusively uses **Ethicon-designed instruments**, explicitly positioned to carry over "decades" of laparoscopic and open-surgery design experience so the tactile/handling experience is more continuous with traditional technique than a from-scratch robotic instrument line — this is a direct competitive argument against da Vinci's proprietary EndoWrist line.

**Software / data layer — what survived from Verily's original pitch.** Verily's founding contribution to Verb Surgical was framed around what the company called **"Surgery 4.0"**: five technology pillars — robotics, advanced visualization, advanced instrumentation, data analytics, and connectivity — aimed at combining Google-style machine vision/ML with surgical hardware, with an explicit access mission (lowering costs and training barriers to expand surgical access globally) ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medical-devices/verb-s-huennekens-unveils-vision-next-gen-robotic-surgery); [MedDevice Online](https://www.meddeviceonline.com/doc/j-j-google-joint-venture-verb-surgical-unveils-digital-surgery-prototype-to-partners-0001)).

After Verily's 2020 exit, that vision persists in a narrower, J&J-branded form: Ottava connects to J&J MedTech's **Polyphonic digital ecosystem**, which aggregates "insights, video, telemetry, and procedural data" across Ottava and J&J's broader surgical portfolio, offering postoperative access to synchronized case video, metrics, and robotic data for analysis and research. This is a materially smaller ambition than Verily's original "machine-vision-driven surgery" pitch — it's a data/analytics layer bolted onto a hardware platform, not an ML-native surgical assistant. No autonomous or AI-guided surgical functions have been cleared or disclosed; Chief Scientific Officer Peter Schulam described the goal only as integrating "data and insights across the surgical ecosystem" ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-receives-fda-market-authorization-in-the-u-s-for-its-ottava-robotic-surgical-system)).

**Other design touches.** In a July 2026 interview, J&J MedTech's Global Head of Robotics & Digital R&D, Neda Cvijetic, discussed additional human-factors design choices baked into the system — folding-arm mechanics, ambient "soothing sounds," and haptic feedback capability — aimed at OR ergonomics and surgeon comfort, though detailed technical specs (haptic force ranges, camera resolution, etc.) have not been published ([Medical Design & Outsourcing](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/jnj-robotics-rd-ottava-folding-arms-soothing-sounds-and-haptics-capability/)).

## Use-Cases / Deployments in Depth

**FDA IDE and first clinical use (2025).** J&J received FDA Investigational Device Exemption approval in 2024 and completed its first clinical cases in 2025. The very first case was announced April 14, 2025: **Dr. Erik Wilson**, Chief of Minimally Invasive and Elective General Surgery at UT Health Houston and lead investigator for the clinical program, performed a **Roux-en-Y gastric bypass** using Ottava at **Memorial Hermann–Texas Medical Center** ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-medtech-announces-completion-of-first-cases-with-ottava-robotic-surgical-system)).

**Clinical study: FORTE (NCT06709261).** A single-arm, open-label, prospective study of **30 patients across six U.S. sites**, evaluating Ottava for Roux-en-Y gastric bypass with a 30-day follow-up. Results were presented as an abstract at the American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery (ASMBS) 2026 annual meeting: the device **met all primary safety and performance endpoints**, with patients averaging about 30 lbs (13.6 kg) of weight loss post-surgery. This trial, combined with preclinical data, formed the basis of the De Novo submission ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/jj-ottava-surgical-robot-gastric-bypass-surgery-study/)).

**FDA De Novo authorization — July 22, 2026.** The FDA granted De Novo marketing authorization for Ottava as a table-integrated soft-tissue robotic system, covering **ten general-surgery procedure types**: Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, gastrectomy, cholecystectomy, splenectomy, gastric sleeve, small bowel resection, appendectomy, lysis of adhesions, fundoplication, and hiatal hernia repair ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-receives-fda-market-authorization-in-the-u-s-for-its-ottava-robotic-surgical-system)). An **additional U.S. clinical study for inguinal hernia repair is currently underway**, indicating the indication set will keep expanding within general surgery — cardiac, thoracic, and urologic indications are not part of any disclosed pipeline for this platform.

**Commercial rollout.** J&J describes a deliberately narrow initial launch: **"select customers"** in the U.S., specifically targeting **high-volume hospitals and health systems with established robotic-surgery programs**, before expanding domestically and then internationally. No specific hospital/health-system names, unit counts, or geographic rollout schedule have been disclosed publicly as of this writing.

## Sales & Commercial Strategy

- **Pricing philosophy:** Undisclosed. J&J has explicitly declined to share placement numbers, price points, or per-unit/service revenue targets, in contrast to Intuitive's long-established capital-plus-instrument-and-service annuity model.
- **Positioning vs. Intuitive:** J&J frames Ottava's differentiation around OR footprint (30–50% smaller), table integration (no separate cart to store/dock), workflow speed (Twin Motion), and instrument continuity with existing Ethicon laparoscopic tools. EVP/Worldwide Chairman of MedTech **Tim Schmid** called it "the start of the next era in surgery," while Company Group Chair for Surgery **Hani Abouhalka** was more measured: "what defined the last 25 years will not define the next 25" — an implicit acknowledgment that da Vinci defined the category ([J&J press release](https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-receives-fda-market-authorization-in-the-u-s-for-its-ottava-robotic-surgical-system)).
- **Analyst reaction is skeptical on near-term share shift.** Leerink Partners and J.P. Morgan both flagged that Ottava arrives roughly 5–7+ years later than J&J's original ambitions, well behind Intuitive's ~25-year head start and installed base of thousands of da Vinci systems, and behind Medtronic's Hugo (EU-approved 2021, U.S. approval targeted ahead of Ottava). J.P. Morgan noted the feature set will appeal to surgeons but that high hospital switching costs and existing da Vinci commitments will blunt near-term competitive impact; analysts see Ottava as a more credible threat to Medtronic's Hugo in ambulatory surgery centers than to Intuitive's hospital base ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/jj-ottava-robotic-surgery-intuitive/699272/)).
- J&J itself has guided that Ottava will not be a **meaningful contributor to MedTech growth until 2028**, consistent with a slow, evidence-led rollout rather than an aggressive share grab.

## Cardiac Surgery Relevance: **NOT APPLICABLE**

Ottava's FDA authorization, clinical trial program (FORTE and the ongoing inguinal hernia study), and every disclosed roadmap item are confined to **general/abdominal soft-tissue surgery**. There is no cardiac, thoracic, or coronary indication cleared, in trials, or publicly discussed for this platform. **Da Vinci (Intuitive Surgical) remains the only robotic platform with an established, credentialed role in robotic coronary bypass and other cardiac procedures.**

## Market Position vs. Competitors

- **Da Vinci (Intuitive Surgical):** Dominant incumbent, ~25-year track record, thousands of installed systems worldwide, proven and credentialed for cardiac, thoracic, and abdominal robotic surgery.
- **Hugo (Medtronic):** EU-approved since 2021; ahead of Ottava on U.S. timeline for some indications; seen by analysts as Ottava's more direct near-term competitor, especially in ASCs.
- **Ottava (J&J):** Newest FDA entrant (July 2026); soft-tissue general-surgery specialist only; differentiates on OR footprint and table integration rather than indication breadth; commercial scale not expected before 2028.

## Key Takeaway for Cardiac Programs

Ottava is not, and shows no signs of becoming in the near term, a cardiac robotics competitor. Its entire clinical and regulatory story — from Verb Surgical's 2015 founding through the 2020 Verily exit, the 2021 $900M delay writedown, first surgery in 2025, and De Novo clearance in July 2026 — is about general/abdominal soft-tissue surgery. For a robotic coronary bypass program evaluation, Ottava is irrelevant to the decision tree today; da Vinci remains the only viable platform choice for cardiac applications.
